Clinical computer use

Computer use for natural-language control of clinical software

Dennis EschweilerDaniel Truhn

Lab for Artificial Intelligence in Medicine  ·  November 2026

Abstract

Hospital information systems rarely offer programmatic access, but every one of them has a screen. Computer-use agents operate software the way a person does: they read screenshots and use mouse and keyboard, following instructions in natural language. In a preliminary study of three off-the-shelf agents in HospitalRun, an open-source hospital information system, single interactions were reliable, while multi-step workflows across several screens failed far more often, mostly through planning errors. The harness described here is our follow-up. It splits the agent in two: a supervisor that owns the plan but never sees the screen, and an actioner that sees the screen but only ever one step of the plan. A step counts as done only when its result is found on screen, next to the right field label, and a check after every action catches values typed into the wrong field. The harness works with any vision-capable model behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint and needs no access to the application beyond its screen.

Work in progress Updates will follow, among them an evaluation on the study tasks and the integration of a decision model.

How it works

Models decide what to do in the hospital system. Deterministic checks decide whether an entry landed where it should.

  1. 1Perceive

    Deterministic

    Text recognition and an icon detector turn each screenshot into elements with stable IDs, plus a list of what the last action changed.

  2. 2Plan

    Supervisor · language model

    Turns the clinician's request into a to-do list, one to three steps ahead, extended as new screens appear.

  3. 3Act

    Actioner · vision model

    Works on one item at a time, with only the patient details it needs, on the live screenshot.

  4. 4Verify

    Deterministic

    An entry counts as done only when its value sits next to the right field label. A value in the wrong field raises an alarm.

  5. 5Escalate

    Supervisor

    Called only when an item is done or blocked, its budget runs out, an alarm fires or the screen stalls.

Steps 2 to 5 repeat until no item is open and the final screen passes the same check.

Where off-the-shelf agents struggle

Our starting point: three off-the-shelf computer-use agents on eight routine tasks in HospitalRun with synthetic patient data, ten runs per task and agent.

  • Basic tasks98%success, such as changing a single field
  • Multi-step workflows69%success, such as requesting a medication
  • Main cause of failurePlanningin 29 of the 40 unsuccessful runs, out of 240 in total; the other 11 were visual perception errors

From our manuscript, currently under review; numbers may still change. The task prompts are in tasks.json.
